## Who needs a crown?
@id: who
@visual: none

A crown goes over a tooth that is cracked, broken or damaged, and covers it.

A bridge fills the gap where a tooth is missing. The teeth either side carry it, which is why more than one tooth is prepared.

After a root canal, a cap goes over the tooth. That is what holds the tooth together afterwards.

## What happens in the chair
@id: process
@sub: Two visits, with the lab in between.
@visual: interior

At the first visit, about 45 minutes to an hour, the tooth is shaped, an impression or scan is taken, and a temporary crown is fitted.

The lab then makes the permanent one, usually in five to ten days.

At the second visit, about half an hour, the crown is tried in, the fit and the bite are adjusted, and it is cemented.

A bridge takes the same two visits, with more than one tooth prepared.

If you want to see the tooth Dr Abaid is working on, the camera puts it on the screen beside you.

## PFM or zirconia?
@id: materials
@visual: none
@note: Never soften or remove the dark-line sentence. Naming the cheaper option's drawback is deliberate.

PFM is a metal shell with porcelain layered over it. It is strong, it is the more affordable of the two, and over time a thin dark line can show at the gum.

Zirconia is one solid ceramic block, with no metal. It is closer to a real tooth to look at, and it costs more.

Where the tooth sits usually settles it. Back teeth are less visible, which leans towards PFM. A front tooth leans towards zirconia.
